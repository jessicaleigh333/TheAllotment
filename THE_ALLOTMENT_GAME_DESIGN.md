# The Allotment — Game Design
Status: Living design reference. Updated 2026-10-11.

## Purpose
The Allotment is a beautiful, welcoming, softly stylized 3D gardening and social game. Players should be able to create extraordinary gardens through time and effort, without needing to spend real money. This document records design decisions and intended features; it is **not** proof that a feature is already implemented.

## Art direction
- Warm, lush, polished, storybook-like 3D environments and characters; prioritize beauty and cohesive art direction.
- Use the previously approved Tea House / Louise character imagery as an avatar-style reference when supplied.
- The example 3D rose bush generated by Codex was approved as a quality/style direction, but should not be assumed to be integrated into the game.
- Prefer modular, reusable, performant models with suitable levels of detail and sensible collision.

## Neighborhoods and plots
- The French garden neighborhood is an early focus. Each neighborhood contains nine plots.
- Standard plots are approximately 1.84 acres; premium plots approximately 3.67 acres (design targets; verify actual in-game scale).
- Assign randomized terrain and special feature selections deterministically when a neighborhood is created, using saved seeds/metadata.
- Players should not be able to preview or identify which plots have rare features before discovery.
- Support spacious gardens with meaningful open planting space, accessible paths, and sensible terrain/collision rules.
- Allow full-scale, substantial features (including major grottos, ruins and monumental landscape elements) even on standard plots where layout permits; do not arbitrarily reduce everything to tiny ornaments.
- Players should be able to garden among and around substantial landmarks.

## French landscape authenticity
Ground French-neighborhood designs in real French garden and landscape precedents rather than generic fantasy:
- Villandry: ornamental kitchen gardens / formal potagers.
- Versailles: formal bosquets, sculpture, waterworks, monumental composition.
- Buttes-Chaumont: grottos, rockwork, picturesque terrain.
- Giverny: romantic flowering garden compositions.
These are **inspirations**, not instructions to copy protected contemporary artwork or mislabel invented features as historical.

## Gameplay and community goals
- Gardening and growth should reward attention, patience and skill.
- Friends may help tend a garden to prevent inactivity, but cannot take over every owner-only action (for example, new planting and trimming invasive climbing vines are intended to remain owner responsibilities).
- Neglected climbing plants can spread gradually on realistic timescales; some inexpensive wooden decorations can deteriorate without upkeep.
- Plan multiple distinctive climate/ecosystem regions over time, beginning with a manageable subset.
- Seasonal world events and themed decorations (Easter, Halloween, Christmas) may encourage return visits.
- Daily login rewards can include decorative items. Players who do not spend real money must still have fair routes to attractive gardens and earnable decor.
- Social/team activities and short, skill-based minigames are planned; make controls approachable on touch and desktop, with precise timing and meaningful mastery.
- A detailed, inclusive avatar creator is planned, not an immediate implementation priority.

2. **Modular French stone walls and gates** — straight, corner, end and entrance pieces.
3. **Stone pathway modules** — reuse existing pathway work, add bends and intersections.
4. **Grass/ground-cover variants** — improve existing grass without breaking terrain.
5. **Rose bush variants** — build on the approved sample if present in repo.
6. **Garden benches and small furnishings** — stone, wood and wrought iron.
7. **Climbing rose trellises and arches** — staged growth variants.
8. **French fountain modules** — formal water features with scalable effects.
9. **Garden utility props** — terracotta pots, watering cans, baskets, tools.
10. **Large landmarks** — monumental grottos, ruins, substantial rockwork and formal garden features.

Asset status is **unverified** until inspected in the actual repository/project. Do not mark an asset integrated solely because it was generated.

## Development practices
- Build iteratively in Godot, with reusable scenes/assets and testable increments.
- Keep gameplay/world scene changes separate from experimental asset work when practical.
- Do not assume all concepts above are already coded.
